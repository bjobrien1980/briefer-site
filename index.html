Rio unionisation and investment claim
Western Mine Workers Alliance · collected 16 August 2026
Displayed publication date unavailable
Graphic + full post text
Full text of union post

RIO warns about unionisation in the Pilbara - but backs in Iron Ore futures all in the same week.

Rio Iron Ore chief Matthew Holcz was reported last week as saying that the re-unionisation of the Pilbara may have an impact on future investment.

But last week at the Melbourne Mining Club in Melbourne Town Hall, he also said despite ongoing demand about China’s maturing steel industry and rising scrap steel usage, the demise of iron ore is “very much being exaggerated”.

While China is approaching peak steelmaking capacity, Holcz said Rio Tinto expects Chinese iron ore consumption to remain relatively stable through to 2030, with some decline after that.

Meanwhile, the firm is betting that growth in emerging markets is expected to offset some of that pressure.

Whilst the contradiction can be hard to understand for outsiders, workers know that actions always speak louder than words.

Rio workers are watching the comapny spend billions in the Pilbara on their New Hope Downs and Greater Brockman sites, as well as the expansion of the West Ang mine sites.

You can read up more here: https://www.investordaily.com.au/rio-tinto-bets-on-india.../

Are you exposed to toxic chemicals at work?

WHS Ministers have rejected or delayed safer exposure limits for 9 chemicals, some of which are onsite across Pilbara mines:

Benzene

Chlorine

Copper

Formaldehyde

Hydrogen cyanide

Hydrogen sulphide

Nitrogen dioxide

Silica dust (RCS)

Titanium dioxide

We’re fighting for safer limits — but that won’t change unless we can show how these chemicals are actually being managed on Australian worksites.

Please take a few minutes to complete our survey and tell us what’s really going on at work. The more examples we have, the stronger our case. Every response is confidential, and we won’t share your details with anybody.

FIFO suicide-prevention campaign
Western Mine Workers Alliance · collected 16 August 2026
Displayed publication date unavailable
Graphic + full post text
Full text of union post

Suicide Prevention is about people and in Australia, we lose about nine people to suicide every day - that's nine people too many. Unfortunately, the resource sector and FIFO workers are disproportionally represented in these numbers.

Suicide prevention is everyone’s business, and your union takes it seriously.

Our members work long hours in gruelling conditions, away from family and friends, and often face a workplace culture that tells many to simply “harden up” or “push through”. That culture is costing lives.

WMWA partner unions the AWU and MEU play an important role. We are a trusted resource for workers, we are on the ground and we hear the quiet conversations.

Unions are not a replacement for clinical or crisis services, but we are often the first conversation, and we know the resource sector has a problem.

This isn’t about making tough industries soft. It’s about making them strong as a mentally healthy workforce is a safer workforce.

Suicide prevention must be built into the safety system - because every worker deserves to come home in one piece, body and mind.

If somethings not right, or you just need someone to talk to, your GP is always a good place to start. Otherwise there are plenty of good confidential services, like Lifeline 131114 or Yarn on 139276.
